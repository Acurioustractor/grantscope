# WA ACCO Transition Pipeline: primary-source research inventory

**Date:** 2026-09-27  
**Status:** Primary-source inventory complete; several implementation details remain unpublished  
**Scope:** ACCO Transition Pipeline, WA Closing the Gap 2026-28, expenditure and procurement sources, justice reinvestment and justice data, Yamatji On-Country priorities, and relevant ACCO governance.  
**Source rule:** WA Government, WA Parliament, WA statutory offices, CASWA, YMAC, JRNA, Olabud Doogethu, ORIC, and other source-owning organisations. The supplied First Nations News article is assessed as the claim set, not used as verification.

## Executive finding

The opportunity described in the supplied article is real and is substantially supported by the official **WA Closing the Gap 2026-28 Implementation Plan**. The WA Government has committed **$5.8 million** to a two-phase ACCO Transition Pipeline under Priority Reform 2:

1. map current service contracts, assess ACCO market readiness, and develop a transition blueprint; and
2. pilot an ACCO Strengthening Program for long-term human-services delivery.

The official plan adds important detail omitted from the article. More than **$3.3 million** of the pipeline allocation is reserved for the Strengthening Program from 2027, after phase one. CASWA is funded to participate in the mapping and identify service requirements. The Strengthening Program will be designed with input from a new Alliance of the Aboriginal Advisory Council of WA (AACWA), Aboriginal Health Council of WA (AHCWA), and CASWA.

The article is not a sufficient implementation source on its own. No public pipeline guidelines, request for proposal, delivery timetable, contract inventory, data specification, selection criteria, regional pilot list, or responsible officer was located. The official plan names **current service contracts** and **human service sectors**, which is narrower than a general map of all government procurement.

The clearest CivicGraph/GrantScope opportunity is therefore not a generic grants search or immediate sales campaign. It is a governed evidence layer connecting public service-contract data, Aboriginal expenditure, ACCO identity, place, funding, justice indicators, contract timing, and evidence provenance. CASWA and the relevant regional/community authorities must define readiness, usefulness, permissible data, and action. JRNA and JusticeHub are relevant where a community chooses justice reinvestment as a service-transition domain. Goods on Country is relevant only as an invited, separately governed enterprise, assets, logistics, housing, or procurement workstream.

## Evidence status definitions

- **Verified:** stated directly in a current first-party source.
- **Partially verified:** the core claim is supported, but wording, scope, attribution, or detail differs.
- **Inferred:** a reasonable connection across sources, but not stated by the source owner.
- **Unknown:** no first-party evidence located, or the operational detail is not yet public.

## 1. ACCO Transition Pipeline and Closing the Gap plan

### Authoritative sources

| Source | Owner | Availability | What it establishes |

| [Whole-of-Government ACCO Strategy](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2024-05/accostrategy.pdf) | WA Government and CASWA | Public PDF | Longer-term policy basis for service transition, sustainable ACCO funding, capability, community control, and AER-led reprioritisation. |

### Verified program structure

- **Total pipeline investment:** $5.8 million.
- **Phase one:** map current service contracts, assess ACCO market readiness, and produce a transition blueprint.
- **Phase two:** pilot an ACCO Strengthening Program for long-term delivery in human service sectors.
- **Phase-two topics:** service design and pricing, strategic planning, workforce development, compliance, and partnership development.
- **Sequencing:** more than $3.3 million is available from 2027 after phase one is completed.
- **CASWA role:** funded participation in mapping and identification of service requirements.
- **Governance link:** the ACCO Strengthening Program is to be designed with input from the AACWA-AHCWA-CASWA Alliance.
- **Related evidence and policy:** Aboriginal Expenditure Review, State Commissioning Strategy, and Aboriginal Business Capacity Building Program.

### Related 2026-28 investments

These are separate but operationally relevant to the pipeline:

| Initiative | Funding | Relevance |
|---|---:|---|
| AACWA-AHCWA-CASWA Alliance | $1.0m | Oversight, accountability, shared decision-making, and approval of regional governance frameworks. |
| Regional Implementation Plans | $2.0m | Place-based plans with locally determined Aboriginal governance and decision-making. |
| Community-Led Data Connection Project | $0.8m | CASWA and the Office of Digital Government will create a culturally responsive access point for government-held data and address privacy and cultural barriers. |
| CASWA sustainability | More than $8.0m, described separately from the $10.1m initiative package | Supports CASWA operations, membership, data functions, and ACCO directory capability over and beyond the plan period. |

### Not yet public

- The phase-one commissioning body, procurement method, request documents, evaluation criteria, or contract award.
- The inventory of service contracts that will be mapped.
- Definitions or measures for “ACCO market readiness.”
- The transition blueprint schema, publication status, and ownership.
- Priority service sectors, agencies, regions, or first pilot cohort.
- How ACCOs will express interest, correct data, refuse inclusion, or review a readiness assessment.
- Whether outputs will be public, shared only with CASWA/Alliance members, or held inside government.
- A named pipeline contact or public implementation timetable beyond phase-two availability from 2027.

## 2. Aboriginal Expenditure Review

### Authoritative sources

- [Western Australian Aboriginal Expenditure Review 2023-24](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/western-australian-aboriginal-expenditure-review-2023-24)
- The same page provides the full PDF, a community guide, and an **XLSX program list**, making this the most immediately reusable official data source for CivicGraph.

### Verified coverage and limits

- The AER is WA's annual overview of State Government expenditure on programs and services specifically for, or predominantly used by, Aboriginal people.
- The 2023-24 publication covers 23 agencies and reports whether Aboriginal organisations were involved, delivery location, and Closing the Gap alignment.
- It reports 2021-22 to 2023-24, with 2022-23 as the focus year.
- Reported Aboriginal expenditure was $759.4 million in 2022-23, up 22.8 per cent from 2021-22.
- Aboriginal organisations were involved in 53.2 per cent of expenditure, either with other parties or as sole provider.
- Six agencies accounted for 80 per cent: Communities, Health, Education, Justice, Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions, and the Mental Health Commission.

The AER is program-level expenditure reporting, not a complete contract register. “Aboriginal organisation involved” does not by itself identify the prime contractor, amount retained by an ACCO, subcontracting structure, contract term, readiness, community authority, or service outcomes. Its program-list XLSX should be preserved as a dated official source and joined to contracts only through evidence-backed agency, program, provider, place, and period keys.

### Disclosure rules that matter to the pipeline

- Award details are required for contracts worth **$50,000 or more**, including agency, description, commencement and expiry, estimated value, successful supplier and procurement method.
- Open advertising is generally required at **$250,000 or more**, but Aboriginal-business direct sourcing, CUAs, exceptions and other methods mean the forward plan and open-tender list are not the whole market.
- The APP applies to goods, services, community services and works at $50,000 or more and has included ACCOs since 1 December 2023.
- The 2024-25 APP report says 383 contracts worth $442 million were awarded to Aboriginal suppliers, 6.82 per cent of all reported contracts.
- Auditor General caveats include missing or late Tenders WA entries, exclusion of contracts below $50,000, grants, many subcontracts, some panel activity, and incomplete variation/expenditure visibility.

### CivicGraph acquisition implication

Tenders WA is registered in GrantScope's `source_frontier` as an enabled procurement source. On 27 September 2026, CivicGraph preserved and ingested all 240 contracts in the portal's public recently-awarded window, with stable portal IDs, detail records, suppliers, values, procurement methods, dates, regions and source hashes. The window covered awards from 27 August to 24 September 2026 and recorded $222.7 million in original contract value. This is verified current-window ingestion, not historical completeness. GrantScope's large `austender_contracts` table remains Commonwealth procurement and cannot substitute for WA State contracts.

The public historical contract search was also tested against its real POST interface. A one-month and a one-week search both returned 49 rows with the portal's explicit warning that results had been reduced. A single-day test for 3 August 2026 returned 16 rows without a reduction warning. Historical acquisition must therefore use bounded daily windows, preserve the portal's result message and reject any reduced result before fetching details or writing contracts. Some unusually busy individual days may still require a second partition by agency or another published filter.

The first applied historical range covered 1 to 3 August 2026. All three daily searches were complete, including a valid zero-contract day. Nineteen contracts were discovered, their detail pages were preserved and hashed, and all 19 were upserted by stable Tenders WA source ID. This increased the stored WA State contract inventory from 240 to 259 while the current recently-awarded window remained separately fixed at 240.

The verified historical range now extends continuously from 1 March to 26 August 2026. All 179 daily windows are complete, with no reduced or failed days. The range contributes 2,643 fully detailed historical contracts, bringing the stored Tenders WA inventory to 2,883 contracts with 2,883 distinct portal IDs and $5.987 billion in recorded original contract value. Supplier names are present for 2,882 records; the single unknown remains explicit. The current recently-awarded window remains separately recorded as 240 contracts so historical growth does not alter current-window reporting or map calculations.

The APP performance collection is now preserved separately as seven annual publication pages and 12 linked PDF artifacts, totalling 64,025,267 bytes. Each file has a SHA-256 fingerprint. These documents support aggregate policy-performance analysis but do not replace award-level Tenders WA records or prove realised expenditure and community outcomes.

A defensible WA contract layer needs dated raw snapshots, stable source IDs, clear separation of opportunity/award/variation/expenditure records, pagination coverage, buyer and supplier resolution, and explicit completeness notes. Power BI sources should be treated as reports unless an authorised, stable extraction path is established.

## 6. Yamatji On-Country Call to Action

### Authoritative sources

- [Yamatji On-Country hub](https://www.ymac.org.au/events/yamatji-on-country/), maintained by Yamatji Marlpa Aboriginal Corporation.
- [2024 Yamatji On-Country Call to Action Statement](https://www.ymac.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/2024-Yamatji-on-Country-Call-to-Action.pdf).
- [2024 meeting report](https://www.ymac.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/2024-Yamatji-On-Country-Meeting-Report.pdf).
- [YMAC submissions index](https://www.ymac.org.au/advocacy-and-law-reform/submissions/) for earlier statements.

### Verified governance and priorities

- Yamatji On-Country is an annual forum created by and for First Nations people across the Mid West, Murchison and Gascoyne and coordinated by YMAC.
- Day one is for First Nations people and organisations to discuss and make decisions. Day two includes invited government and other decision-makers.
- The call to action covers systemic change, racism, housing and accommodation, police and justice, education, employment/training/economic development, and health and wellbeing.
- The statement is explicitly intended as a tool for ACCOs planning programs, seeking funding and advocating for change.
- The Mid West Aboriginal Organisations Alliance (MAOA) accepted a community invitation to steer action and government accountability. In 2025 the WA Government announced $300,000 to strengthen MAOA's regional peak role.

### Relationship to the pipeline

The Yamatji call to action is a strong community-authored source for regional priorities and engagement protocol. It supports the case for place-based contract and service mapping in the Mid West, Murchison and Gascoyne. However, the official implementation plan does not state that the statewide ACCO Transition Pipeline was created in direct response to the 2025 call to action. That relationship should remain **inferred/claimed by the article** until CASWA, YMAC, MAOA or WA Government publishes the direct link.

The latest publicly linked full statement found in this review is the 2024 consolidated statement. YMAC says it undertook further consultations in 2025 and updated the call to action, but a stable public 2025 PDF was not located. Use the current YMAC hub as the authority for the latest status.

## 8. Relevance to the four bodies of work

### CivicGraph / GrantScope

Best fit: a provenance-rich public evidence inventory that can answer:

- Which human-service contracts exist, which are ending, and which agencies buy them?
- Which expenditure programs involve Aboriginal organisations, in what places, and under what provider arrangement?
- Which organisations are legally identifiable and publicly self-described as community controlled?
- Which transition opportunities are official, proposed, inferred, or unknown?
- Which regional priorities and governance bodies must be consulted before any action?

The platform should keep contract opportunity, grant/funding, organisation identity, community authority, service evidence, and outreach permission as separate records. A model-generated readiness score would be inappropriate without CASWA/ACCO definition, correction rights, context, and human review.

### JRNA

Best fit: offer transparent evidence infrastructure under JRNA and participating-community governance for a justice-specific pilot. Useful inputs could include public contract/expenditure data, justice indicators, local service directories, inspection findings and community-defined outcomes. JRNA and community partners should define place, categories, permissible data, success, publication, and withdrawal.

### JusticeHub

Best fit: make the justice subset legible without pretending that funding causes outcomes. A WA evidence view could connect official outcome trends, oversight reports, public programs, service contracts, ACCO providers, and justice-reinvestment initiatives. Olabud Doogethu and Old Ways New Ways are potential evidence cases only with first-party review and permission for any deeper representation.

### Goods on Country

Best fit: a separate, invited practical workstream where an ACCO or regional authority identifies a need involving household goods, beds, local production, logistics, procurement readiness, paid work, circular materials, housing support, or enterprise infrastructure.

Goods on Country should not be used as the lead justification for engaging CASWA, MAOA, YMAC, JRNA or ACCOs. Public procurement signals are not demand, community permission, a purchase order, or authority to represent a place. Any Goods pathway must separately verify the buyer, community authority, need, quantity, specification, funding/payment route, delivery responsibility, ownership, proceeds, and approval to contact.

## 9. Recommended engagement sequence

1. **CASWA discovery conversation:** ask whether a small public-source map would assist its pipeline role and data office, what must remain outside the map, and who should govern definitions and corrections.
2. **Regional authority check:** if the Mid West/Murchison/Gascoyne is proposed, approach through YMAC/MAOA and ask whether the Yamatji priorities and current governance support the work.
3. **Justice governance check:** involve JRNA, Social Reinvestment WA and the relevant community-led initiative only if justice is selected as a domain.
4. **Public-data prototype:** one region, one service family, public sources only, with provenance and unknowns visible. No readiness scoring and no automated outreach.
5. **Community/peak review:** review usefulness, errors, absent context, access rules and next steps before adding non-public data or making introductions.

A simple opening proposition is:

> We have mapped the public evidence that could support the ACCO Transition Pipeline, including service-contract disclosures, Aboriginal expenditure, organisation identity, justice data and regional priorities. Before building anything further, we want to ask whether a small, community-governed evidence map would be useful, what it must not do, and who should hold the definitions and decisions.

## Evidence limits

- This review establishes public-source availability as at 27 September 2026. Portals, office holders, programs and dashboards can change.
- The 2026-28 Implementation Plan was downloaded from the official WA Government URL and text-extracted because the indexed web reader could not render the full large PDF.
- No Tenders WA account-only documents, agency-only procurement reports, non-public pipeline records, Justice microdata, CASWA member records, or community-held data were accessed.
- GrantScope database counts are point-in-time diagnostics. They do not establish completeness, current ACCO status, authority, unique expenditure, eligibility, readiness, outcomes or permission to contact.
- The supplied article remains useful as a discovery lead. Where it differs from the official plan, the official source wording and scope should control.

## Entity-resolution and open-data continuation

The procurement records are now being resolved against the graph already held by CivicGraph rather than waiting for supplier ABNs that the public WA files do not publish.

- The two WA sources contain 17,055 contracts and 20,283 separate supplier components after panels are split.
- A deterministic resolver links supplier names and parenthetical legal/trading names to CivicGraph canonical entities, ACNC names and aliases, ORIC names and social-enterprise names. Registry names only become entity evidence through an existing ABN, ACN or ORIC ICN link.
- The first committed pass produced 3,048 unique entity links, including 803 registry-backed links; 223 supplier components have more than one exact entity candidate and remain ambiguous; 17,012 remain unmatched.
- Within the 283-record transition queue, 65 contracts now have one contract-level entity link and 24 carry a community-controlled discovery signal.
- Within the 54-record Kimberley queue, 13 contracts have one contract-level entity link and 6 carry a community-controlled discovery signal.
- Multi-supplier contracts retain component-level matches but do not receive one forced contract-level entity. Review decisions are preserved across reruns.
- Fuzzy matching is deliberately deferred to a review-only lane. It is not required for the deterministic ledger to commit and cannot silently turn similarity into identity.

The official Data WA CKAN catalogue is also now scanned through a repeatable search manifest. The first run found 150 distinct catalogue datasets across twelve search themes, of which 116 expose at least one machine-readable resource. The manifest is held at `data/wa-open-data/discovery.json` with dataset IDs, publishers, licences, update dates, resource URLs, formats, matched queries and deterministic fit signals.

The strongest immediately usable additions are the already-ingested annual Tenders WA CSVs, the child-protection Safety and Wellbeing Assessment CSV, and authoritative place layers such as Aboriginal Communities and Town Reserves and Aboriginal Settlements. Spatial and cultural-heritage layers must not be treated as community permission, and child-protection aggregates must not be treated as provider performance or causal evidence.

The first bounded identity review then resolved five high-priority Kimberley procurement names through sourced aliases: OVAHS, BRAMS, KAMS, Marnin Bowa Dumbara and Fitzroy Valley Men's Shed/Gurama Yani U. The evidence combines the published procurement name with ORIC, ACNC or first-party organisational material and retains the legal ABN or ICN link. This lifted the transition queue to 71 contract-level entity links and 28 community-controlled signals. The Kimberley queue now contains 18 contract-level entity links and 10 community-controlled signals. These remain identity and discovery signals, not readiness or engagement decisions.

A subsequent resolver audit found two implementation gaps. Parenthetical legal and trading names were not being extracted because the SQL regex had been double-escaped, and the shared organisation normalizer used a word-boundary form that did not remove legal suffix variants in PostgreSQL. The corrected WA-local normalizer is deliberately conservative: it removes punctuation and legal suffixes but retains substantive organisation words, and any base name shared by more than one entity remains ambiguous.

After correction, 7,054 of 20,283 supplier components have unique deterministic entity links, 1,570 are explicitly ambiguous and 11,659 remain unmatched. No fuzzy links are included. The transition queue contains 149 contract-level entity links, while the Kimberley queue contains 24 of 54. Eleven Kimberley contracts carry a community-controlled discovery signal. Newly recovered service-provider identities include OVIS/Pat Thomas House, Inclusion WA, Palmerston, Centrecare, DADAA, Hope, Ruah and Yorganop. Yorganop's current first-party site explicitly describes the incorporated association as an ACCO, providing current evidence beyond its historical ORIC record.
