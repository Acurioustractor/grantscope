# NSW Department of Communities and Justice — social procurement evidence

**Prepared:** 2026-08-08 · **Source:** public contract disclosures, `tenders.nsw.gov.au` via the Open
Contracting Data Standard, matched by ABN against the CivicGraph social and Indigenous enterprise registry.
**Every figure here traces to a queryable row.** See `one-pager.provenance.md`.

## The headline

**91 social and Indigenous enterprises hold 383 disclosed contracts with DCJ, worth $3.69 billion.
84 of those contracts are still running today.**

That is lifetime contract value across a 2008–2025 window, not annual spend. Most of the value sits in
long multi-year human-services agreements.

## The gap this leaves — and it is the interesting part

The smallest disclosed contract in the whole set is exactly **$150,000**, because that is the NSW
disclosure threshold. Every engagement below it is invisible in public data.

The NSW Social Enterprise Policy allows agencies to **directly engage a social enterprise under $150K**
without a competitive process. So the easiest lane in NSW policy is precisely the lane no public record
can evidence. DCJ cannot currently show its own sub-threshold social procurement, and neither can anyone
else. That is a reporting gap, not a performance one, and it is fixable.

## Read this before quoting any number

- **$3.69B is lifetime contract value, not annual spend.** Multi-year agreements are counted at full value
  in the year they start.
- **Coverage ends January 2025.** The 2023–2025 figures are incomplete because of disclosure publishing
  lag, so the apparent decline in recent years is a data artifact and must not be presented as a trend.
- **"Social enterprise" here means presence in our registry with an external mark.** It is our definition,
  not DCJ's, and we say so.
- **Sub-$150K activity is absent, not zero.** See above.
